3. KEY ANALYTICAL TAKEAWAYS & EVIDENCE-BASED INSIGHTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Strongest Predictors of Treatment Seeking:
  1. Work Interference (Cramer's V = 0.542, p = 1.30e-63): Employees who report work interference ('Often', 'Sometimes') seek treatment at dramatically higher rates (>80% for Often) than those reporting 'Never'.
  2. Family History (Cramer's V = 0.375, p = 1.16e-40): Individuals with a family history of mental illness are significantly more likely to seek treatment (75.5% vs 34.0%).
  3. Employer Care Options & Benefits (Cramer's V = 0.272 and 0.223, p < 10^-14): Knowledge of available care options and coverage directly correlates with higher treatment uptake.
- Gender Differences (Cramer's V = 0.198, p = 7.44e-12): Female (68.8%) and Non-Binary/Other (76.2%) respondents seek treatment at higher rates compared to Male respondents (45.1%).
- Workplace Variables Not Reaching Statistical Significance:
  - Company Size (p = 0.1188): Company headcount alone does not dictate treatment rate.
  - Tech vs Non-Tech (p = 0.2958): Working specifically for a tech firm shows no significant difference.
  - Remote Work (p = 0.3712): Remote workers seek treatment at comparable rates to in-office workers.
  - Self-Employment (p = 0.5238): Treatment proportion is virtually identical between self-employed and employed.
